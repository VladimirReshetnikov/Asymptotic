\begin{abstract}
This audit examines the current Asymptotic repository against its existing nine-review register, emphasizing additional evidence rather than repeating the backlog. Four successful native evaluations expose distinct failures at contract boundaries. An ambient assumption can turn an excluded source interval into a positive inverse certificate. Composition can capture a fixed parameter as its new expansion variable and return $1+\OO(a^2)$ for a function identically equal to $1/2$. A real opaque function with a correctly supplied first derivative can receive a false magnitude remainder because formal Taylor generation imports an unstated analyticity assumption. Finally, direct expansion and an observable route admit a provably nonreal symbolic coefficient that scalar addition and inverse construction correctly reject.

These findings do not show that the package's specialized coefficient formulas are generally incorrect. They show that correct local algebra is insufficient when proof context, parameter quantifiers, regularity, or codomain evidence changes between operations. The article gives source locations, observed outputs, exact witnesses, narrowly scoped repairs, acceptance tests, and performance-conscious implementation proposals. It also compares the package with the actual breadth of Wolfram's native asymptotic facilities, rather than treating those facilities as merely Taylor-series tools. A source-pinned certificate-patch generator, native reproducers, desired-contract tests, independent checks, and evidence manifests accompany the article.
\end{abstract}

\section{Executive assessment}
\subsection{What should change first}
The first repair priority is the certificate's proof context. A positive \code{Certified} flag should not depend on an unrelated notebook-level assumption about the source variable. The next priority is composition's treatment of fixed parameters: a successful composition must not silently turn a parameter-dependent bound into a uniform one. The ordinary forward fallback then needs an explicit regularity admission rule, and public real-valued operations need consistent codomain admission.

\subsection{What remains valuable in the design}
The package exposes a useful abstraction beyond a displayed approximation: a \code{GeneralizedSeries} carries a local coordinate, finite coefficients, a remainder description, assumptions, and, in several families, source equations and evidence for later operations. The ordinary sparse power--log engine is complemented by exact-core inversions, finite flat sectors, Fourier-polynomial logarithmic coefficients, reciprocal-logarithmic calculus, and Gamma/Barnes inverse scales. These are cooperating specialized algebras, not a single universal transseries field.\cite{readme,core,operations,flat,fourier,reciprocal,gammainverse,barnesinverse}

The inspected special-function machinery also contains substantive safeguards. Exact identities are separated from truncated asymptotic formulas; half-integer modified Bessel identities retain both exponentials when required. Finite flat-sector inversion keeps the zero sector exact instead of disguising an algebraically truncated inverse as an exact core. The inverse-Gamma operation code explicitly prevents source replay from improving uncertainty inherited from an operand. These choices are worth preserving while repairing the newly demonstrated boundaries.\cite{identities,flat,gammaops}

The resulting recommendation is not a rewrite of the coefficient engines. It is a small set of stronger admission and transport rules around them, with explicit proof obligations and regression cases that make the repaired behavior observable.

\section{The mathematical and software layers being compared}
\subsection{Ordinary jets and exact carriers}
Write $u\to0^+$ for the package's positive local coordinate. The ordinary representation is structurally a finite sparse jet
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinaryjet}
 J(u)=\sum_{\beta\in B}u^\beta P_\beta(\log u)
       +\OO\!\left(u^\rho(1+|\log u|)^d\right),
\end{equation}
where the ordered source weights are exact real numbers and the coefficients are logarithmic polynomials. Internally, a precision-tracked triple stores the rows and the pair $(\rho,d)$. Equal weights are merged before the relevant truncation operations. The derived-series layer can retain an exact carrier and offset,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carrier}
 b(y)+C(y)\bigl(J(w(y))\bigr),\qquad w(y)>0,
\end{equation}
so that the jet's order can be relative to $C$ rather than an absolute power of $y$.\cite{core,operations}

These representations make algebraic precision transport practical. For example, if $A$ has leading power $\alpha$ and error power $P$, while $B$ has leading power $\beta$ and error power $Q$, multiplication must account for errors at powers $P+\beta$, $Q+\alpha$, and, when relevant, $P+Q$. A precision representation cannot be inferred merely by multiplying the printed finite parts. This is mathematical reasoning, independent of any particular implementation.

The four findings concern assumptions around such algebra. In N02 the hidden constant in \eqref{eq:ordinaryjet} is allowed to depend on a parameter that later varies. In N03 the existence of the stated error class was never established. Correct exponent arithmetic cannot repair either loss of information.

\subsection{Specialized families have different coefficient domains}
The flat engine keeps exponential sector degree separate from inner algebraic precision. Its operation layer propagates the omitted exponential tail separately from discarded coefficients inside retained sectors. The Fourier engine instead uses finite modes $P_\omega(\log u)e^{i\omega\log u}$ and checks conjugacy to represent real coefficients. Its error envelope uses coefficient magnitudes, not a possibly vanishing oscillatory factor.\cite{flat,flatops,fourier}

The reciprocal-logarithmic operations use a carrier times a Taylor jet in a positive reciprocal logarithm. Gamma and Barnes inverse expansions keep complete polynomials in an inverse logarithm at each power of an exact large inverse core. Their source formulas and declared Poincare remainder contracts differ from a convergent Taylor model. General operations explicitly refuse several unsupported combinations rather than converting every family into an ordinary power--log jet.\cite{reciprocal,gammainverse,barnesinverse,gammaops,operations}

This specialization is an architectural strength when the restrictions are explicit. A refusal because an operation requires a larger coefficient algebra is not an incorrect result. The dangerous case is a successful conversion that silently changes the meaning of an existing bound or proof.

\section{Comparison with built-in Wolfram Language}
\subsection{The native baseline is broader than Taylor reversion}
Wolfram's \code{Series} supports negative and fractional powers, logarithms, expansions at infinity, and more than univariate Taylor-polynomial output. Its parameter and regularity assumptions matter. \code{Asymptotic} is a separate general asymptotic interface, while \code{AsymptoticSolve} addresses implicit equations and systems with automatically inferred scales. Comparing only with \code{InverseSeries} would therefore understate the native baseline.\cite{wlseries,wlasymptotic,wlasolve}

The first four rows are grounded in the native reference descriptions and inspected package representation/operation code; the last two are comparisons of responsibilities rather than claims of a universal native limitation.\cite{wlseries,wlseriesdata,wlinverse,wlcompose,wlasymptotic,wlasolve,operations,native,certificates}

\subsection{Representation is not the same question as computability}
\code{SeriesData[x,x0,coefficients,nmin,nmax,den]} represents its displayed powers using the rational lattice determined by its integer indices and denominator. This is a representation constraint. It does not imply that Wolfram Language cannot manipulate irrational powers in expressions or compute asymptotics involving them through other interfaces.\cite{wlseriesdata}

The package's sparse exact-real support is therefore a genuine representation advantage for its selected algebra, but not proof of an exclusive computational capability. The fair comparison is whether a given task produces the needed coefficients, branch, usable residual, and retained precision contract, and whether later operations preserve that contract. The supplied optional comparison harness records individual outputs without converting a refusal into a claim that the native system can never solve the mathematical problem.

\subsection{Orders must be aligned before comparing answers}
A native \code{AsymptoticSolve} order is an approximation-order request and need not equal polynomial degree; its documentation describes error relative to the final scale term. The package, depending on family, uses an exclusive exponent cutoff or an inclusive sector depth. Equal integers in two calls do not automatically request equal information.\cite{wlasolve,flat,fourier,gammainverse}

For an ordinary comparison such as $f(x)=x+x^2$, one should align the actual retained powers, not just pass the same integer to both interfaces. For a Gamma inverse comparison, align the exact large core and the retained inverse-logarithmic coefficient blocks. For a flat expansion, state separately the number of exponential sectors and any coefficient truncation. This is a comparison protocol, not a newly reported order-API defect.

\subsection{Documented defaults become obligations at the wrapper boundary}
Two native defaults are directly relevant. \code{Refine} appends its default assumption context to positional assumptions unless an explicit assumption option replaces it. \code{Series} defaults to treating unrecognized functions as analytic. Those are documented native behaviors, not themselves Wolfram defects. A wrapper promising a source-domain certificate or a magnitude remainder must account for them.\cite{wlrefine,wlseries}

The key distinction is the strength of the advertised result. A symbolic Taylor expression under a convention about unknown functions, a Poincare approximation under stated hypotheses, and a verified enclosure for a constrained source root are different outputs. The wrapper may rely on native algorithms, but it must not silently promote one kind of evidence into another.

\section{N01: ambient assumptions can create a false branch certificate}\label{sec:n01}
\subsection{A small public reproducer}
All examples below use the pinned package loaded in a fresh kernel. The fully qualified forms are retained to avoid dependence on the reader's context path.
\begin{lstlisting}
Clear[x, y];
s = AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticInverse[
  ConditionalExpression[x, x > 2],
  {x, Infinity}, {y, 2}];

clean = AsymptoticInverse`InverseCertificate[
  s, 1, "Interval" -> {1/2, 3/2},
  "Center" -> 1, "MaxRefinements" -> 0];

ambient = Assuming[x > 2,
  AsymptoticInverse`InverseCertificate[
    s, 1, "Interval" -> {1/2, 3/2},
    "Center" -> 1, "MaxRefinements" -> 0]];
\end{lstlisting}
The object is constructed \emph{before} entering \code{Assuming}. It retains the source condition $x>2$, the expression $y$, and \code{Assumptions -> True}. The clean certificate correctly returns \code{Failure["OutsideBranch",...]} and records that the source condition was not proved on the entire closed verification interval.

Under the ambient assumption, the observed output instead contains:
\begin{lstlisting}
<|"Certified" -> True,
  "RootEnclosure" -> {1, 1},
  "CertifiedSourceDomain" -> x > 2,
  "SourceDomainVerified" -> True,
  "CertifiedFunction" -> x,
  "CertifiedTarget" -> 1|>
\end{lstlisting}
These fields are transcribed from the successful native response; the full surrounding diagnostic material is not necessary to demonstrate the contradiction.

\subsection{What is false, precisely}
The unrestricted equation $x=1$ has root $1$. The numerical root is not the defect. The specified inverse source domain is $x>2$, on which the equation has no solution. Moreover, the entire interval $[1/2,3/2]$ is outside that domain. Thus the displayed root enclosure and the displayed positive source-domain claim cannot both describe the requested constrained inverse.

This is not saved by interpreting the ambient assumption as another ordinary parameter hypothesis. The certificate is quantifying over an explicit verification interval in the source variable. It cannot use the desired source predicate itself as an unrecorded premise to declare that the interval satisfies the predicate. The source variable must be treated as bound by the interval verification, not as a free fixed parameter whose value is already known to exceed two.

\subsection{The exact source-level cause}
The current \code{certPositiveCondition} begins with:
\begin{lstlisting}
c = TimeConstrained[
  Quiet[Refine[condition, Element[x, Reals]]],
  1, condition];
Which[c === True, True, c === False, False, ...]
\end{lstlisting}
The remaining helper has substantial interval logic: Boolean decomposition, exact affine endpoint evaluation, and rational enclosure comparisons. That machinery is bypassed when the first simplification has already reduced the predicate to \code{True}.\cite{certificates}

The independent native primitive check returned
\begin{lstlisting}
Assuming[x > 2, Refine[x > 2, Element[x, Reals]]]
(* True *)

Refine[x > 2, Element[x, Reals], Assumptions -> True]
(* x > 2 *)
\end{lstlisting}
This matches the official assumption semantics.\cite{wlrefine}

\subsection{Narrow repair and its limits}
The minimal source candidate changes that call to:
\begin{lstlisting}
Refine[condition, Element[x, Reals], Assumptions -> True]
\end{lstlisting}
It preserves the intended realness normalization while preventing this specific call from importing \code{$Assumptions}. The supplied \code{make_certificate_patch.py} requires the exact canonical module blob, requires one matching anchor, and writes a separate candidate, unified diff, and manifest. It never overwrites the input.

This candidate has \emph{not} been successfully tested end-to-end in the native kernel here. The isolated \code{Refine} behavior was observed, and the Python source-editing mechanics passed fixture tests. Those facts are weaker than a patched certificate passing its positive and negative integration cases. The generator also does not fix ambient assumptions imported earlier by other constructors or arbitrary evaluation side effects.

The broader repair is to make the proof environment explicit at certificate entry. Only retained parameter hypotheses appropriate to the certificate may participate; source predicates must be proved on the interval. A source-symbol alpha-renaming can reduce accidental name collision, but explicit assumption isolation is still necessary: renaming alone is not a principled proof-context boundary.

\subsection{A correct interval obligation}
For a source domain $D(x)$ and verification interval $I=[l,r]$, the relevant premise is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervaldomain}
 \forall x\in I,\quad D(x).
\end{equation}
For an affine inequality $ax+b>0$ with exact rational coefficients, its range is obtained at the endpoints. In this case the range of $x-2$ on $I$ is $[-3/2,-1/2]$, immediately refuting \eqref{eq:intervaldomain}. No symbolic assumption solver is required.

A useful repair therefore also reorders the helper: evaluate exact rational Boolean and affine cases first; use isolated symbolic normalization only where identities such as $\operatorname{Im}x=0$ need it. This is a targeted performance opportunity because it avoids unnecessary symbolic work on the newly critical path. No timing improvement is claimed without measurement.

\subsection{Acceptance criteria}
The contaminated and clean calls for target $1$ must both fail to certify the constrained root. A valid control with target $3$, interval $[5/2,7/2]$, and center $3$ should still be certifiable. Adding an unrelated ambient source assumption must not turn an invalid interval into a valid one, nor change a valid rational proof into an unrelated branch conclusion. The valid target-$3$ control is supplied as a regression specification and checked mathematically, but was not successfully executed through the native connector during this audit.

\section{N02: composition promotes a fixed-parameter bound to a false uniform bound}\label{sec:n02}
\subsection{The observed public result}
\begin{lstlisting}
Clear[x, a];
outer = AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticExpansion[
  x/(a + x), {x, 0, 2}, Assumptions -> a > 0];
inner = AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticExpansion[
  a, {a, 0, 4}];
r = AsymptoticInverse`SeriesCompose[outer, inner];
\end{lstlisting}
The outer object contains $x/a+\OO(x^2)$ for fixed $a>0$. The inner object is exactly $a$. The observed composition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:badcompose}
 1+\OO(a^2),\qquad a\to0^+,
\end{equation}
with \code{RemainderScaleExpression -> a^2}. However, direct substitution into the original function gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{a}{a+a}=\frac12.
\end{equation}
The error is the nonzero constant $1/2$. This is a genuinely false asymptotic statement, not a conservative or suboptimal bound.

All quantities are real on the recorded positive domains. The inner variable approaches the correct positive source endpoint. Additional sign checks or a stronger proof of $a>0$ cannot fix this example. What changed is that $a$, formerly a fixed parameter of the outer germ, became the variable of the new asymptotic approach.

\subsection{The missing quantifier}
For fixed $a>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remainderexact}
 \frac{x}{a+x}-\frac{x}{a}=-\frac{x^2}{a(a+x)}.
\end{equation}
As $x\to0^+$ this is indeed $\OO_a(x^2)$. The subscript matters. The assertion has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixedquantifier}
 \forall a>0\;\exists C(a),\delta(a)>0\;\forall x\in(0,\delta(a)):
 |R(x,a)|\le C(a)x^2.
\end{equation}
It does not provide constants uniform as $a\to0$. On the diagonal $x=a$, the effective ratio $|R|/x^2=1/(2a^2)$ diverges. A condition that parameters are positive does not imply that constants depending on them remain bounded.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:diagonal}
For $f(x,a)=x/(a+x)$ and $a>0$, substitution of $x=a$ into the finite approximation $x/a$ yields a constant error $1/2$. For any integer $m>1$, substitution of $x=a^m$ yields an error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separatederror}
 E_m(a)=\frac{a^{2m-2}}{1+a^{m-1}},
\end{equation}
which is not $\OO(a^{2m})$ as $a\to0^+$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The diagonal identity is immediate. For $x=a^m$ the exact value is
$a^{m-1}/(1+a^{m-1})$, whereas the retained approximation is $a^{m-1}$.
Their difference is \eqref{eq:separatederror}. Dividing by $a^{2m}$ gives
$1/[a^2(1+a^{m-1})]\to\infty$.
\end{proof}
Only the $m=1$ public composition was successfully evaluated natively. The $m>1$ family is an independently proved extension with exact-rational Python checks, not an additional claimed native observation. It is informative because $x/a\to0$ when $m>1$: even moving into the genuine small-ratio regime does not justify the overstrong transported error.

\subsection{Where the representation loses the dependency}
The generic \code{SeriesCompose} computes the outer local coordinate on the inner jet, finds its valuation $\alpha$, composes retained coefficient rows, and transports the outer error pair $(p,d)$ to $(\alpha p,d)$. The relevant operation effectively adds a pure remainder at power \code{alpha p}. There is no fixed-parameter dependency check at this point.\cite{operations}

Retained coefficients that depend on the new variable can be handled as expressions by the coefficient application machinery. The old remainder constant has no such expression: its possible dependence on $a$ was not retained as an explicit quantitative factor. This creates an asymmetry. The finite part is evaluated along the new path, while the unknown error is treated as though all old parameters remained fixed.

A symbol clash is therefore a semantic event, not a harmless naming issue. Renaming the outer source variable is appropriate binder hygiene, but renaming the free parameter $a$ would change the user's function. The correct response is to detect the dependency and either establish its admissibility or refuse the unsupported composition.

\subsection{Immediate repair without a general uniform-asymptotics engine}
A narrow safe rule is:
\begin{quote}
When the outer operand has an unknown remainder, reject a composition whose new expansion variable occurs as a fixed parameter of that outer operand, unless retained uniformity or quantitative error evidence authorizes the new dependence.
\end{quote}
The dependency footprint must include the original source, stored assumptions, domain conditions, and error provenance. Scanning only the displayed finite coefficients is insufficient: a parameter may first occur in an omitted term. For instance, the finite approximation to $x^2/(a+x)$ below exponent two is zero for fixed $a$, yet the omitted coefficient depends singularly on $a$.

This rule does not require banning all compositions with shared symbols. If the outer object is exact, there is no unknown outer tail to promote. The exact outer function $x/a$ composed with the exact inner function $a$ legitimately gives $1$ on $a>0$. This control belongs alongside the failing partial-outer example so that a fix does not indiscriminately reject harmless exact algebra.

The first diagnostic should identify the captured parameter and the failed obligation, for example:
\begin{lstlisting}
Failure["NonuniformParameterCapture", <|
  "CapturedParameters" -> {a},
  "OuterApproachVariable" -> x,
  "NewApproachVariable" -> a,
  "RequiredEvidence" -> "Uniform outer remainder or transported majorant"
|>]
\end{lstlisting}
This is a proposed diagnostic, not an existing package result.

\subsection{A quantitative route that really works}
From \eqref{eq:remainderexact}, positivity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:majorant}
 |R(x,a)|\le \frac{x^2}{a^2}\qquad(x,a>0).
\end{equation}
Unlike the bare symbol $\OO_a(x^2)$, this majorant can be substituted. Along $x=a^m$, it becomes $a^{2m-2}$, the correct error power in Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal}. Along $x=a$, it becomes $1$, correctly indicating that this finite approximation has no vanishing error on that path.

An eventual implementation could retain a majorant expression together with its domain and threshold dependence. Alternatively, a source-aware operation can first substitute into the exact retained source and then construct a fresh expansion of the resulting function. That yields $1/2$ exactly in the observed example. Such a route should explicitly say it recomputed from the source; it is not a consequence of the old finite jet alone.

The distinction prevents another UX failure: an object with retained source data must not make operations appear to gain precision from nowhere. ``Compose these approximations'' and ``compose the exact sources and re-expand'' are different operations with different cost and information requirements.

\section{N03: a truthful finite jet does not establish an analytic remainder}\label{sec:n03}
\subsection{A real opaque function with a correct derivative}
Define a real function near zero by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gdef}
 g(0)=0,\qquad
 g(x)=x^2\log\bigl(1+|\log|x||\bigr)\quad(x\ne0).
\end{equation}
The native reproducer deliberately supplies its numeric definition and its correct first derivative at zero:
\begin{lstlisting}
Clear[g, x];
g[t_?NumericQ] := If[TrueQ[t == 0], 0,
  t^2 Log[1 + Abs[Log[Abs[t]]]]];
g'[0] = 0;

s = AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticExpansion[g[x], {x, 0, 2}];
\end{lstlisting}
The observed result has finite expression zero and
\begin{lstlisting}
"Remainder" -> PowerLogRemainder[x, 2, 0]
"Exact" -> False
"Assumptions" -> True
\end{lstlisting}
The coefficient data are not fraudulent or inconsistent. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 g'(0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{g(h)-g(0)}h
      =\lim_{h\to0}h\log\bigl(1+|\log|h||\bigr)=0.
\end{equation}
The function is continuously differentiable near zero. It is the stronger second-order magnitude remainder that fails.

\subsection{Exact contradiction with the advertised bound}
For $0<x<1$, write $L=-\log x$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g(x)}{x^2}=\log(1+L)\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
Hence $g\notin\OO(x^2)$. The native evaluator also returned \code{Infinity} for the equivalent scaled limit \code{Limit[Log[1-Log[x]],x->0,Direction->"FromAbove"]}.

The invalid remainder can look deceptively plausible numerically because its missing factor grows very slowly. At $x=e^{-k}$, the error ratio is only $\log(1+k)$. Sampling a few small values is much weaker than this exact limit. The Python checks use the scaled expression directly to avoid underflow; they illustrate the witness but are not the proof of its limiting behavior.

The example is stronger than supplying an arbitrary unsupported symbolic head. It is a real function with an executable numeric implementation and correct finite derivative data, and the package returns a successful magnitude-bearing object. A caller should not have to infer an unstated analyticity premise from the implementation's fallback strategy.

\subsection{How a formal default crosses the boundary}
In the ordinary core, \code{fwdAnalytic} asks native \code{Series} for a local expansion of a unary function, while \code{fwdSeries} is a general fallback. The ordinary observable path also uses a native Taylor expansion for regular unary expressions. These inspected calls do not themselves turn off the default analytic treatment of an unrecognized head.\cite{core,operations}

Wolfram documents \code{Analytic -> True} as the default controlling whether unknown functions are treated as analytic. This is useful for formal symbolic work. It is insufficient evidence for the concrete magnitude interpretation of \code{PowerLogRemainder} when the user's function is not analytic.\cite{wlseries}

The architecture already distinguishes some stronger cases: specialized native-function handling has its own guarded parsing and real-domain machinery. The finding is not that every adapter ignores regularity. It is that the generic fallback can admit a weaker formal result into an object whose downstream operations treat the remainder as an actual bound.\cite{native,realdomain}

\subsection{Repair: make regularity admission explicit}
The strict fallback should not infer analyticity merely from an unknown function's ability to produce a symbolic \code{SeriesData}. A first hardening candidate is to request \code{Analytic -> False} at generic import points and decline results whose remaining form cannot be admitted under the supported analytic model. This change must be tested with the known-function cases rather than applied as an unexamined global option mutation.

A deliberate formal mode can remain useful. It should retain something like
\begin{lstlisting}
"RegularityEvidence" -> <|
  "Origin" -> "UserDeclaredAnalyticGerm",
  "Function" -> g,
  "Point" -> 0,
  "Scope" -> "Local neighborhood"
|>
\end{lstlisting}
The spelling is a design proposal. The essential requirement is that a formal premise remain visible and that certificate consumers not mistake a user assertion for an independently proved analytic fact.

Analyticity is sufficient but not always necessary. A bounded second derivative on a neighborhood would justify a second-order Taylor magnitude remainder in this example's role. More generally, the admission layer should accept the particular differentiability and derivative-bound evidence an operation needs. A finite table of derivatives at one point is not such neighborhood evidence.

No native integration result is claimed for these repairs. Attempts to evaluate strict-mode and patched controls encountered connector failures. The supplied desired-contract test permits either rejection or a genuinely sufficient conservative remainder; it does not prescribe one exact implementation response.

\subsection{Consequences for tests and API explanations}
The regression should retain the truthful derivative definition. Replacing the example with a deliberately false derivative would test user inconsistency, not the package's regularity inference. A known analytic function such as $e^x$ must remain a positive control, and a returned unknown-function expansion must say which regularity premise was used.

A diagnostic such as ``Unable to establish a magnitude remainder for this opaque function'' is more informative than ``Unsupported input.'' It tells the caller that the missing item is not a coefficient but an admissibility condition. The package can still provide a formal jet as a different kind of result, provided its consumers respect the distinction.

\section{N04: codomain admission is inconsistent across public routes}\label{sec:n04}
\subsection{Observed acceptance and rejection controls}
Under $a>0$, the principal value $\sqrt{-a}=i\sqrt a$ is provably nonreal. The following direct expansion nevertheless succeeds:
\begin{lstlisting}
AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticExpansion[
  Sqrt[-a] + x, {x, 0, 2}, Assumptions -> a > 0]
\end{lstlisting}
Its expression is $\sqrt{-a}+x$, its remainder is zero, and its recorded target domain is $x>0$. The same successful result is obtained by applying an additive observable:
\begin{lstlisting}
base = AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticExpansion[
  x, {x, 0, 3}, Assumptions -> a > 0];
AsymptoticInverse`SeriesObservable[base, z + Sqrt[-a], z]
\end{lstlisting}
By contrast, the native controls
\begin{lstlisting}
AsymptoticInverse`SeriesAdd[base, Sqrt[-a]]
AsymptoticInverse`AsymptoticInverse[
  x + Sqrt[-a] x^2, {x, 0}, {y, 3}, Assumptions -> a > 0]
\end{lstlisting}
correctly return \code{Failure["UnprovedRealCoefficient",...]}.

The inverse control is important. It would be inaccurate to claim that the ordinary inverse constructor generally lacks the real-coefficient guard. The demonstrated gap is in direct/independent coefficient admission and its relation to other public routes.

\subsection{Why syntactic exactness is not a realness proof}
A symbolic expression can be exact and contain no literal \code{Complex} object while still being nonreal under the supplied assumptions. Testing for machine numbers or explicit complex atoms does not prove $c\in\RR$.

The ordinary constant representation \code{pConst} checks the logarithmic polynomial shape and zero status, but does not establish realness on its own. The forward dispatcher and independent-coefficient application routes can reach it directly. Other public scalar routes separately prove real coefficients and reject this example.\cite{core,operations}

The result $x+i\sqrt a$ is a perfectly meaningful exact complex expression. What is missing is a coherent contract saying that this route intentionally constructs a complex-valued germ, with compatible downstream operations. The inspected package describes real-branch computation, and its existing scalar/inverse guards make the accidental discrepancy visible.\cite{readme,core,operations}

\subsection{Repair at the correct abstraction level}
The shared low-level coefficient machinery should not simply be modified to reject every complex intermediate. Fourier representations naturally use complex modes whose conjugacy yields a real function, and other native transformations may temporarily introduce complex algebra.\cite{fourier,native}

Instead, every public real-valued constructor or operation should pass a common admission boundary after its representation is normalized. That boundary needs three outcomes: proved real, proved nonreal, and unresolved under the stated assumptions. The last two may both decline in a strict real-only mode, but diagnostics should distinguish them when possible.

A reusable coefficient check should be keyed by the normalized coefficient, the explicit assumptions, the coordinate domain, and the requested codomain. It should be evaluated once per distinct coefficient within an operation, not repeatedly in every sparse product. This both closes the route discrepancy and avoids turning validation into a large new symbolic cost.

The API-level invariant is straightforward: explicit \code{SeriesAdd[s,c]} and the mathematically equivalent additive observable must not disagree on whether a provably nonreal constant is allowed. A future complex-valued mode could make both succeed, but that would require explicit codomain and branch/error rules. Merely allowing one path to slip through is not that feature.

\section{A repair architecture focused on the demonstrated defects}
\subsection{Separate four kinds of context}
The examples reveal four independent items that should not be merged into one undifferentiated assumption expression:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C=(\Gamma_{\rm param},\ D_{\rm source},\ A_{\rm approach},\ H_{\rm regularity}).
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma_{\rm param}$ describes fixed parameters, $D_{\rm source}$ is the source domain to be checked, $A_{\rm approach}$ describes the limiting variable and side, and $H_{\rm regularity}$ describes analytic or derivative-bound evidence. A codomain judgment and a fixed-parameter footprint accompany the representation.

This need not require changing the public wrapper head. The association payload can gain explicit evidence records, while existing coefficient engines receive the same normalized jets. The important change is that operation admission examines these records before interpreting successful algebra as a successful analytic result.

For a supported function $F$, a useful internal judgment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:judgment}
 (\mathcal C,\mathcal P)\vdash F=J+R,\qquad |R|\preceq B,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P$ identifies fixed parameters and $B$ is either a qualitative scale with stated dependence or a quantitative majorant. The symbol $\preceq$ must say whether constants and thresholds may depend on $\mathcal P$.

N01 improperly uses $D_{\rm source}$ as though it were an ambient premise proving itself on an interval. N02 changes $\mathcal P$ without revalidating $B$. N03 creates \eqref{eq:judgment} from a formal coefficient model without $H_{\rm regularity}$. N04 omits a codomain premise on some public routes. This classification gives repair locations more precise than ``validate everything.''

\subsection{An implementable sequence, not a monolithic rewrite}
First isolate the certificate domain normalization and add the invalid-interval regression. Next add a conservative dependency check to partial-outer composition. Both changes can be local and fail closed on exactly the newly identified unsupported cases.

Then add generic regularity admission at native fallback boundaries, while retaining explicit known-family paths. Finally centralize public codomain admission and route-equivalence tests. These changes can be reviewed independently; they should not be hidden inside an unrelated optimization or a new special-function family.

Only after those guards are stable is it necessary to add richer positive evidence: transported parameter-dependent majorants, user-declared analytic germs, and source-backed re-expansion. This ordering produces correct refusals immediately while keeping the eventual richer behavior grounded in a clear obligation.

\subsection{Proof caching must retain its dependencies}
Several proposed repairs invite caching, but a cache keyed only by a coefficient or predicate is unsafe. The same expression can be real under one parameter domain and nonreal under another; a uniformity statement can hold on a compact parameter set and fail near a singular boundary. An ambient assumption must never become a hidden cache dependency.

A per-request cache is a practical first implementation. Its key contains normalized expressions and an immutable explicit context identifier. Public operations construct the identifier from recorded evidence, not from a dynamically consulted global assumption variable. Cross-request reuse can be considered later only when the evidence and parameter bindings are stable.

For N01, no cached Boolean claim that $x>2$ is true may stand in for evaluation on a different interval. For N02, a cached coefficient expansion at fixed $a$ cannot authorize the diagonal path. The same discipline that prevents these errors also makes profiling interpretable: cache hits then mean reuse of the same mathematical obligation.

\subsection{Do not solve a metadata problem by silently recomputing everything}
The exact source retained by an object is valuable, but repeatedly invoking it can hide a broken approximation contract. In N02, recomputing the exact diagonal source gives the right answer. In a general object, the source may be expensive, unavailable, a user declaration, or only a truncated model. A source-aware operation must disclose its origin and preserve any uncertainty in that source.

Consequently a result should distinguish coefficient algebra performed on the existing approximation from reconstruction using stronger source information. The latter may legitimately improve precision, but that improvement must not be attributed to the former. This is a targeted extension of provenance semantics required by the proposed N02 repair, not a new report of the already catalogued provenance-storage issue.

\section{Performance and UX implications specific to these findings}
\subsection{Four targeted measurements worth adding}
No new wall-clock measurements were obtained, so the following are proposed measurements rather than claimed performance defects or speedups.

For the certificate helper, count calls to symbolic \code{Refine} separately from affine interval tests before and after reordering. A rational interval predicate should take the deterministic path; proof-time failures should report which normalization remained necessary.

For composition, measure the parameter-footprint check before coefficient composition. A stored footprint should make a rejected diagonal case inexpensive. Exact-source replay should be measured separately because it is a different operation, potentially far more costly than transporting a jet.

For regularity admission, count native fallback attempts for opaque functions under strict and declared-analytic modes. A refusal should not repeatedly call the same native expansion at increasing orders when the missing premise is regularity rather than truncation depth.

For realness admission, count distinct normalized coefficients and proof attempts. The useful ratio is proofs per distinct coefficient under the same context, not proofs per generated product row. This identifies whether the new guard is being placed too deep in the arithmetic loop.

These measurements attach directly to N01--N04. They do not reissue the existing register's general term-budget, dense-export, or provenance-growth findings.\cite{status}

\subsection{Error messages should state the lost premise}
N01 should report an interval/domain failure with the retained predicate and verification interval. N02 should identify the captured fixed parameter and the need for a uniform bound or source re-expansion. N03 should distinguish an unavailable analytic/magnitude contract from unavailable coefficients. N04 should report the coefficient and the real-domain obligation that failed.

A successful result should be equally explicit. A user-declared analytic model should not look indistinguishable from a known-family theorem. A source-recomputed composition should say that it used stronger source information. A certificate should state its actual proof assumptions and scope, not merely a global success flag. These are narrowly targeted explanations of the demonstrated gaps.

\subsection{A specific safe-but-unsupported observable opportunity}
The current Gamma/Barnes inverse power route rejects a noninteger outer power whenever the source endpoint is not positive infinity. This is conservative: a negative source branch can create principal-power hazards. The implementation correctly avoids inventing a branch.\cite{gammaops}

There is nevertheless a useful admissible subcase. Suppose $X(y)<0$ is the selected source root and the existing observable is $X(y)^2$. Then its positive square root is
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{X(y)^2}=-X(y),
\end{equation}
not $X(y)$. A safe extension would track the sign of the \emph{observable}, separately from the sign of the source root, and reconstruct a signed source-power observable. Merely deleting the guard and multiplying exponents would be wrong.

For an even integer source power $m$, a real outer power $k$, and a negative real source root, the principal real value is $|X|^{mk}$. When $mk$ is an integer it can be represented as $(-1)^{mk}X^{mk}$; otherwise an explicit positive-magnitude carrier is needed. This is a mathematical design opportunity, not an observed incorrect current result, and no native acceptance test was completed for it. The supplied independent checks cover only the elementary sign identity.

\subsection{Tests should check logical strength, not just finite coefficients}

\section{Exact witnesses and useful variants}
\subsection{Certificate witness without asymptotic numerics}
Let $D=(2,\infty)$, $I=[1/2,3/2]$, and $F(x)=x-1$. Then $F(1)=0$, $1\in I$, $I\cap D=\varnothing$. Any output asserting both a root enclosure inside $I$ and verification of the source predicate throughout $I$ is inconsistent with these exact rational facts. No floating-point accuracy, conditioning, inverse approximation quality, or difficult transcendental inequality is involved.

\subsection{A family of diagonal failures}
For $c>0$, substitution $x=ca$ into $x/(a+x)$ gives $c/(1+c)$, whereas substitution into $x/a$ gives $c$. The error $c^2/(1+c)$ is independent of $a$. This family shows that the observed $1/2$ discrepancy is not a special cancellation at one parameter value.

If the outer expansion retains $N$ geometric terms,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{x}{a+x}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j-1}(x/a)^j
  +\frac{(-1)^N(x/a)^{N+1}}{1+x/a},
\end{equation}
then along $x=ca$ the error remains a nonzero constant for each fixed finite $N$. Increasing the ordinary source cutoff without changing the parameter contract does not solve the issue. Along $x=a^m$, the actual error power is $(m-1)(N+1)$ rather than the naively transported $m(N+1)$. These are mathematical extensions, not additional native measurements.

\subsection{The opaque function's derivative really is continuous}
On $0<x<1$,
\begin{equation}
 g'(x)=2x\log(1-\log x)-\frac{x}{1-\log x}.
\end{equation}
Both terms tend to zero. The definition is even, so the negative-side derivative tends to zero as well. Thus the supplied derivative value at zero is compatible with a $C^1$ function. Its second derivative is unbounded near zero, consistent with the failure of an $\OO(x^2)$ first-order Taylor remainder.

The witness separates three statements: the derivative at zero exists; the first-order Taylor remainder is $o(x)$; and that remainder is $\OO(x^2)$. The first two hold, while the third does not. A successful finite Taylor-coefficient computation alone does not bridge that gap.
