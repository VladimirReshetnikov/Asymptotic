\section{Forward expansions}
\label{sec:forward}

Before inverting anything we need the forward function in the normal form $ax^p(1+\sum_ix^{\delta_i}B_i(\log x))$, and the second question \cite{q2} is a forward problem in its own right. This section shows that a large class of expressions has convergent power--log expansions, and describes the precision-tracking evaluator that computes them with a rigorous remainder class.

\subsection{Convergent power--log expansions}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:pl-expansion}
A function $F$ on $(0,w_0)$ has a \emph{convergent power--log expansion} with support in $\beta_0+S$ ($S$ a finitely generated positive semigroup with gaps $\delta_1,\dots,\delta_m$) if there are polynomials $C_\sigma\in\R[L]$ and constants $M,K,d\ge0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 F(w)=\sum_{\sigma\in S}w^{\beta_0+\sigma}C_\sigma(L)\quad\text{for }0<w<w_1,
 \qquad |C_\sigma(L)|\le M\,K^{n(\sigma)}\,\M(w)^{d\,n(\sigma)}\ \ (L\le\log w_1),
 \label{eq:pl-expansion}
\end{equation}
where $n(\sigma)$ is the least $|\bk|$ with $\bk\cdot\bdelta=\sigma$ and the series converges absolutely. We write $F\in\mathcal E$.
\end{definition}

The coefficient bound makes the tail after any cutoff controllable (Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} below), and it is stable under all the operations we need.

\begin{lemma}[Tail of a convergent expansion]
\label{lem:tail}
Let $F\in\mathcal E$ as in \eqref{eq:pl-expansion}, $\delta_*=\min_i\delta_i$, and $H>0$. Then
\begin{equation}
 F(w)-\sum_{\sigma<H}w^{\beta_0+\sigma}C_\sigma(L)=\OO\bigl(w^{\beta_0+H}\M(w)^{Nd}\bigr),\qquad N=\lceil H/\delta_*\rceil.
 \label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
More precisely, the omitted part equals $w^{\beta_0+\sigma_*}C_{\sigma_*}(L)+\oo(w^{\beta_0+\sigma_*})$ where $\sigma_*=\min\{\sigma\in S:\sigma\ge H\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split the omitted terms into those with $n(\sigma)\le N-1$ and the rest. The first group is finite (Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}) and each of its terms is $\OO(w^{\beta_0+H}\M^{(N-1)d})$ since $\sigma\ge H$ and $\deg C_\sigma\le dn(\sigma)$ follows from the bound. For the rest, the number of multi-indices with $|\bk|=n$ is at most $m^n$, and $\sigma\ge n\delta_*$, so the sum is bounded by $Mw^{\beta_0}\sum_{n\ge N}\bigl(mKw^{\delta_*}\M^d\bigr)^n=\OO(w^{\beta_0+N\delta_*}\M^{Nd})$, and $N\delta_*\ge H$. The refined statement follows by applying this with $H$ replaced by any $H'\in(\sigma_*,\sigma_*+\delta_*)$, which shows that everything beyond $\sigma_*$ is $\OO(w^{\beta_0+H'}\M^{N'd})=\oo(w^{\beta_0+\sigma_*})$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closure]
\label{thm:closure}
The class $\mathcal E$ contains the constants and $w$ and is closed under sums, products, and the following operations, with the supports and gaps indicated.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item If $F\in\mathcal E$ has leading block $cw^\beta$ with a \emph{constant} $c>0$, then $F^a\in\mathcal E$ for every real $a$ and $\log F\in\mathcal E$; the support of $F^a$ is $a\beta+S'$ and that of $\log F$ is $\{0\}\cup S'$ (with the block $\log c+\beta L$ at exponent $0$), where $S'$ is generated by the gaps of $F/(cw^\beta)-1$.
\item If $F\in\mathcal E$ is bounded, i.e.\ $\beta_0\ge0$, and its exponent-$0$ block is $c+kL$ with $k\in\R$, then $e^F\in\mathcal E$ with leading block $e^cw^k$.
\item If $F\in\mathcal E$ has $\beta_0\ge0$ and constant exponent-$0$ block $c$, and $\phi$ is analytic at $c$, then $\phi\circ F\in\mathcal E$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sums and products: the union and the sum of two finitely generated semigroups are finitely generated, absolutely convergent series may be multiplied, and the coefficient bound of a product follows from $\M^{dn}\M^{d'n'}\le\M^{\max(d,d')(n+n')}$ and the finiteness of the number of representations. For (1)--(3) write the relevant part of $F$ as $c\,w^{\beta}(1+U)$ or $c+U$ with $U=\sum_{\sigma>0}w^\sigma C_\sigma(L)$ and $\val U>0$. Group the terms of $U$ by multi-index and logarithmic degree and introduce the finitely many \emph{small parameters} $T_{\bk,j}$ standing for $w^{\bk\cdot\bdelta}L^j$; then $U=\sum_{\bk,j}T_{\bk,j}c_{\bk,j}$ is, for the purposes of the operation, a convergent power series in the $T$'s (this is where the coefficient bound is used: the series in the $T$'s converges in a polydisc $|T_{\bk,j}|<\rho$ because $\sum MK^n\rho^n(d+1)^n<\infty$ for small $\rho$). The map $T\mapsto(1+U)^a$, $\log(1+U)$, $e^U$, or $\phi(c+U)$ is analytic near $T=0$, hence has a convergent multivariate Taylor series whose coefficients obey Cauchy estimates $|c_{\bnu}|\le M'\rho'^{-|\bnu|}$. Substituting $T_{\bk,j}=w^{\bk\cdot\bdelta}L^j$ and regrouping the absolutely convergent double series by total weight gives \eqref{eq:pl-expansion} with the stated support; the number of monomials $\bnu$ contributing to a given multi-index is at most $(d+1)^{|\bnu|}$, which is absorbed into $K$, and each such monomial has logarithmic degree at most $d|\bnu|$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is the analytic backbone of every forward and inverse expansion in this article: an expression built by finitely many of these operations has a convergent expansion, and every truncation is a genuine asymptotic expansion by Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}. The hypotheses of (1)--(3) are also necessary within the power--log class: a non-integer power or a logarithm of a leading block $w^\beta P(L)$ with $\deg P\ge1$, an exponential of an unbounded expression (which produces $e^{-1/w}$-type terms), or an analytic function applied to $c+kL+\cdots$ with $k\ne0$ (which produces $\sin(\log w)$-type oscillations) leave the class (Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}).

\subsection{The precision-tracking evaluator}
\label{sec:evaluator}

The package computes forward expansions by structural recursion over the expression tree, carrying for every subexpression a triple $(T,P,D)$: a jet $T$ and the assertion
\begin{equation}
 \text{subexpression}=T+\OO\bigl(w^P\M(w)^D\bigr),\qquad P\in\R\cup\{+\infty\},
 \label{eq:triple}
\end{equation}
where $P=+\infty$ means that $T$ is exact. A global working order $K_w$ bounds the number of terms of every unit series that is expanded. The rules, all justified by \eqref{eq:O-arithmetic} and Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}, are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item constants and $w$ are exact; \wl{Plus}: $T_1+T_2$ truncated below $\min(P_1,P_2)$, with the degree of the smaller precision (the maximum when the precisions coincide);
\item \wl{Times}: $T_1T_2$ truncated below $\min(P_1+\val T_2,\ P_2+\val T_1)$, with degrees $D_1+\deg_{\mathrm{lead}}T_2$ and $D_2+\deg_{\mathrm{lead}}T_1$ (an empty jet contributes its own $P$ and $D$);
\item \wl{Power} with an exact real exponent $a$: nonnegative integers by repeated multiplication (exact); otherwise the leading block $cw^\beta$ of $T$ must have constant $c>0$ (or $c<0$ with integer $a$); with $U=T/(cw^\beta)-1$, relative precision $P-\beta$ and relative cutoff $c_{\mathrm{rel}}=\min(P-\beta,K_w-a\beta)$, the result is $c^aw^{a\beta}\sum_{k}\binom akU^k$ with precision $a\beta+c_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and degree $\lceil c_{\mathrm{rel}}/\val U\rceil\cdot\deg_{\max}U$ from the truncation of the binomial series (Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} applied to the finite jet $U$), combined with $D$ by \eqref{eq:O-arithmetic};
\item \wl{Log}: the leading block must be $cw^\beta$ with constant $c>0$; the result is $\log c+\beta L+\log(1+U)$;
\item \wl{Exp}: $T$ must have no negative exponents and its exponent-$0$ block must be $c+kL$ with exact numeric $k$; the result is $e^cw^k\exp(U)$;
\item an analytic head $\phi$ (\wl{Sin}, \wl{ArcTan}, \wl{BesselJ[0,\,$\cdot$]}, \dots) applied to $T=c+U$ with constant $c$: $\sum_k\phi^{(k)}(c)U^k/k!$ with the Taylor coefficients obtained from \wl{Series[$\phi$[c + t], \{t, 0, N\}]};
\item any other subexpression is expanded by \wl{Series[expr, \{w, 0, n\}, Assumptions -> w > 0]}; the coefficients (which may contain $\log w$) and the irrational-power monomials that \wl{Series} leaves outside its \wl{SeriesData} are parsed as jets, the precision is the order of the \wl{O} term, and the logarithmic degree $D$ is read from the first omitted block obtained by expanding one order further.
\end{itemize}
The whole expression is re-expanded with a larger $K_w$ until the final precision reaches the requested cutoff. The result is reported as the retained blocks plus $\OO_{\sigma_*,k_*}$, where $\sigma_*$ is the exponent of the first omitted block if one lies below the final precision (its complete coefficient having been computed), and otherwise the precision pair $(P,D)$ itself.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness]
\label{prop:soundness}
If every leaf and every \wl{Series} fallback satisfies its asserted \eqref{eq:triple}, then so does the root, and the reported remainder class is valid.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induction over the tree with \eqref{eq:O-arithmetic} for sums and products, Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} for the truncated unit series (the finite jet $U$ generates a finitely generated semigroup with $\delta_*=\val U$ and $d=\deg_{\max}U$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} makes the unit series a convergent expansion), and the mean value estimate $\Phi(1+U)-\Phi(1+U_{\mathrm{known}})=\OO(U-U_{\mathrm{known}})$ for the contribution of the unknown part of the argument. The final remainder statement is Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}'s refined form.
\end{proof}

The only hypothesis that the package cannot verify is the last bullet: when \wl{Series} is used, the assertion that the omitted part is $\OO(w^\rho\M^k)$ with $k$ the degree of the first omitted block is the assumption that \wl{Series} produces a genuine asymptotic expansion in the power--log scale, which is the case for the functions it handles (analytic functions of $w$, $w^\alpha$ and $\log w$). Exact finite expressions never use the fallback and their remainders are proved.

\subsection{The second question}

For $F(x)=(1+x+x^{\sqrt2})^{\sqrt2}$ at $x\to+\infty$ the local variable is $w=1/x$ and
\[
 F=w^{-\sqrt2}\bigl(1+w^{\sqrt2-1}+w^{\sqrt2}\bigr)^{\sqrt2}=w^{-2}\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{\sqrt2}{k}\bigl(w^{\sqrt2-1}+w^{\sqrt2}\bigr)^k ,
\]
an instance of item (1) of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} with gaps $\sqrt2-1$ and $\sqrt2$. The exponents of $x$ are $2-(\sqrt2-1)k_1-\sqrt2k_2$; ordering the semigroup elements gives \eqref{eq:answer3}, and the first omitted exponent is $7-5\sqrt2$ (multi-index $(5,0)$). The workaround in the question, which peels off one leading term per limit computation, produces the same seven terms; the evaluator produces them in a fraction of a second together with the remainder $\OO(x^{7-5\sqrt2})$, and any number of further terms.
