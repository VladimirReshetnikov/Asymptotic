\section{Incremental and grouped inverse computation}
\label{sec:incremental}

Three changes reduce repeated work without changing the coefficient theorem or
the meaning of a complete power--log block. They concern the ordinary engine
with finitely many positive exact gaps $\delta_i$. Symbolic depth truncation
continues to use the individual multi-index formula.

\subsection{A persistent exact-weight frontier}

Let $I$ be the set of retained multi-indices, initially empty, and let its
candidate frontier initially consist of the zero multi-index. At each step,
select the smallest candidate weight $\sigma$, promote \emph{every} candidate
of that weight into $I$, and add all their one-step successors
$\bk+e_i$ to the candidate set. Candidates are deduplicated by their entire
integer vector; their weights are canonicalized independently.

\begin{proposition}[Complete layers and coefficient reuse]
After the layer at weight $\sigma$ is processed, $I$ is exactly
$\{\bk\in\N^m:\bk\cdot\bdelta\le\sigma\}$, and the candidate set is its
one-step outer boundary. Each retained multi-index has its coefficient
evaluated exactly once. Summing the coefficients of an entire layer before
counting a block preserves all resonances and exact cancellations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every nonzero multi-index has a predecessor of strictly smaller weight because
all gaps are positive. Inductively, every index of the next candidate weight
has already been generated by a processed predecessor. A successor of an index
in the current layer has strictly larger weight, so processing one member of
the layer cannot expose a previously unseen member of the same layer.
Deduplication prevents a second insertion through another predecessor. The
coefficient of each weight is the complete finite sum in
Theorem~\ref{thm:coefficients}, whether or not that sum is zero.
\end{proof}

The implementation returns its state by value. It stores the normalized model,
assumptions, observable power, retained indices, candidate frontier, and merged
blocks. Reusing a state therefore reuses coefficients only for the same model
and assumptions. It does not use a global cache that might accidentally mix
branches or parameter hypotheses. The resource budget counts distinct retained
and candidate indices, as in the original bounded enumeration. Raising a term
goal no longer recomputes coefficients of every earlier layer. A cancellation
may still require arbitrarily many layers before another nonzero block appears;
incremental enumeration does not provide a new termination theorem.

\subsection{Newton iteration with growing precision}

Write $E(U)=0$ for \eqref{eq:normalized}, and let
$\delta_* = \min_i\delta_i$. The zero jet is correct below $\delta_*$.
If $U_s$ is correct strictly below weight $s$, one Newton step performed
strictly below
\[
 h=\min(2s,H),\qquad
 U_h=\left[U_s-\frac{E(U_s)}{E'(U_s)}\right]_{<h},
\]
is correct strictly below $h$. Indeed, $E'(U_s)$ has nonzero constant
coefficient $p$, and the Newton error is quadratic in the old error. Products,
unit powers, and logarithmic composition do not decrease the weight of the
error. The argument uses exponent valuation, with polynomial logarithms as
coefficients; logarithmic degree need not stay bounded as $h$ increases.

The working cutoffs are
$\min(2\delta_*,H),\min(4\delta_*,H),\ldots,H$, rather than $H$ at every
step. Their construction uses exact comparisons, with no floating-point
estimate of the required iteration count. The implementation checks
$[E(U_h)]_{<h}=0$ after each step before recording the new precision. This is
an exact formal consistency check. It is not a numerical error certificate
for a particular nonzero value of $z$.

A reusable Newton state contains its unit jet and certified formal precision.
Refinement to a larger cutoff starts from that jet; a request for a smaller
cutoff leaves the stronger state intact, and a caller may truncate a copy.
The monomial model has the exact unit jet zero and needs no Newton steps.
Other models require a finite cutoff.

\subsection{Combine resonances before applying Euler operators}

Put
\[
 H_1(z,L)=\sum_i z^{\delta_i}B_i(L),\qquad
 H_1(z,L)^n=\sum_\sigma z^\sigma A_{n,\sigma}(L).
\]
The multinomial theorem gives
\[
 A_{n,\sigma}=
 \sum_{\substack{|\bk|=n\\\bk\cdot\bdelta=\sigma}}
 \frac{n!}{\bk!}\,B^{\bk}.
\]
At fixed depth $n$ and weight $\sigma$, all summands of
\eqref{eq:master} have the same differential operator. By linearity, the unit
bracket of $g^r$ can therefore be computed as
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g(y)^r}{z^r}
 =1+\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_\sigma z^\sigma
 \frac{(-1)^n r}{p^n n!}
 \prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(\D+r+\sigma+pj)A_{n,\sigma}(L).
 \label{eq:grouped-lagrange}
\end{equation}
For cutoff $H$, successive sparse products compute
$[H_1^n]_{<H}$. Only $n$ with $n\delta_*<H$ can contribute, so the computation
is finite. Coefficients of equal weight are combined during each sparse
product, before differentiation. Contributions at the same weight but
different depths are then combined in the result; they too can cancel.

Equation~\eqref{eq:grouped-lagrange} is especially useful when the generators
have many additive relations. It does not require commensurable gaps, and it
does not replace the public individual-coefficient formula: that formula
retains the distinct perturbation markers and their provenance. Grouping can
reduce coefficient work without eliminating the separate boundary enumeration
used for remainder reporting. It is not a claim of uniform speedup on every
input; polynomial coefficient growth and sparse multiplication remain material
costs.

The independent engine is selected with \wl{Method -> "GroupedLagrange"}.
The default \wl{"Lagrange"} method keeps the individual formula, so their
agreement remains a direct check of the regrouping. Both use complete exact
weights for term goals; grouping does not change which blocks are requested.

\subsection{Validation obligations}

The regression suite compares every successive incremental region with the
independent bounded multi-index enumerator, checks exact algebraic collisions
and complete cancellations, and verifies the unique-index resource budget.
It compares growing-precision Newton with the individual Lagrange formula for
incommensurable logarithmic gaps, including a negative leading power, and
checks that refinement preserves every previously certified coefficient.
Grouped coefficients are compared with individual coefficients for several
observable powers and with Newton independently. These are formal algebra
checks; runtime measurements and analytic error bounds are separate evidence.

\subsection{Recorded native measurements}

The reproducible script \path{Tests/BenchmarkIncremental.wl} compares frozen
reference control flow with the new helpers and checks exact result equality.
For the public grouped comparison it checks the remainder descriptor as well.
One fresh-kernel run on Wolfram 15.0.1 for Windows recorded:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Fixture & Reference seconds & Updated seconds\\
\midrule
Eight logarithmic blocks, term goal & 0.19092 & 0.05692\\
Newton through twelve weights & 6.62615 & 3.35497\\
Seven-power model, target cutoff twelve & 0.35947 & 0.48142\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first fixture reduces coefficient evaluations from 95 to 31. All three
results agree exactly. The grouped public method is slower on this small
fixture, where normalization, boundary enumeration and sparse products remain
part of the cost. It stays an explicit alternative; these observations do not
justify replacing the default method or asserting a uniform speedup.
