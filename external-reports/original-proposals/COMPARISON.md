# The nine reports: analysis and comparison

Nine research-and-implementation reports on Mathematica Stack Exchange
question 236367 (*How to get an asymptotic of the real-valued branch of the
inverse function?*) arrived on 7 September 2026 as ZIP archives.  They were
unpacked into `01-…` to `09-…` (numbered in the order of the archive names;
the checksum ledgers and the archives themselves were removed).  Each report
contains an article (LaTeX and PDF), a Wolfram Language package, native MUnit
tests, examples and a Python (SymPy/mpmath) verification record.  None of the
nine packages had been executed in a Wolfram kernel by its authors: every
report states that no kernel was available.  This document records what the
reports have in common, where they differ, what each contributes, what is
wrong, and the result of running all nine test suites on Wolfram 15.0.1.

## 1. Common core

All nine reports independently arrive at the same mathematical picture.

- **Model class.** A finite *power–log germ* near `0+`,
  `f(x) = a x^p (1 + Σ_i x^{δ_i} B_i(log x))` with `a, p, δ_i > 0` and real
  polynomials `B_i` (report 07 restricts to `p = 1`).  The two questions'
  examples are `x + x^2 (1 + log x)` (one gap `δ = 1`, `B = 1 + L`) and
  `x + x^√2` (one irrational gap `δ = √2 − 1`, `B = 1`).
- **Branch.** The real branch is fixed by `x > 0`, `y → 0+`, `x / (y/a)^{1/p} → 1`;
  the complex `InverseFunction` output of the question is the Taylor germ at
  the nonzero complex root `z0 = exp(W_{-1}(−e) − 1)` of `1 + x(1 + log x) = 0`
  (reports 05, 08 and 09 compute that germ explicitly).
- **Coefficient formula.** With the uniformizer `z = (y/a)^{1/p}`, `L = log z`,
  `D = d/dL`, and a multi-index `k` of weight `σ = k·δ`, `n = |k|`,

  ```
  g(y)^r = z^r + Σ_{k≠0} z^{r+σ} (-1)^n r / (p^n k!) · Π_{j=1}^{n-1} (D + r + σ + p j) · Π_i B_i(L)^{k_i},
  ```

  proved through the auxiliary-parameter (marker) form of Lagrange–Bürmann
  inversion applied at a positive point, plus the Euler identity
  `d/dz [z^α P(log z)] = z^{α-1} (α + D) P`.  Every report has exactly this
  normalization (sign `(−1)^n`, multi-index factorial `k!`, factor `p^{−n}`,
  operator constants `r + σ + p j`).  Equal weights (resonances) are summed.
- **Support and finiteness.** The inverse exponents lie in `(r + S)/p` where
  `S` is the additive semigroup generated by the gaps; below any bound only
  finitely many multi-indices occur even for incommensurable gaps.
- **Convergence.** The expansion converges for small positive `y` after
  substituting finitely many small parameters `z^{δ_i} (log z)^j`; proofs use
  either the analytic implicit function theorem in those parameters (01, 02,
  04, 08) or Rouché's theorem with Cauchy estimates uniform in `L` (03, 05,
  06, 07, 09), the latter yielding explicit coefficient majorants.
- **Remainder.** Truncating below an exponent cutoff leaves an error
  `O(y^{β*} (1 + |log y|)^{k*})` where `β*` is the first omitted exponent of
  the support; the logarithmic factor cannot be dropped: after
  `y − y^2(1 + log y)` the error is `y^3(2 log^2 y + 5 log y + 3) + …`, not
  `O(y^3)`.
- **Answers.** All reports agree on
  `y − y^2(1+L) + y^3(2L^2+5L+3) − y^4(5L^3 + 41/2 L^2 + 27L + 23/2) + y^5(14L^4 + 241/3 L^3 + 335/2 L^2 + 151L + 299/6) + …`
  (`L = log y`), with top coefficients `(−1)^n C_n` (Catalan numbers), and on
  the Fuss–Catalan law `g(y) = Σ_n (−1)^n/(1+n(α−1)) · C(nα, n) · y^{1+n(α−1)}`
  for `x + x^α`, with `α = √2` giving `y − y^√2 + √2 y^{2√2−1} − (6−√2)/2 y^{3√2−2} + (17√2/3 − 4) y^{4√2−3} + …`.
- **Forward remainders.** If the input is only a truncated expansion of `F`
  with `F − f = O(x^ρ (1+|log x|)^k)` and a matching derivative bound (or
  monotonicity), then `F^{-1} − f^{-1} = O(y^{(ρ−p+1)/p} (1+|log y|)^k)`.
- **Tooling.** All nine represent expansions as sparse lists of
  `{exact exponent, polynomial in a log symbol}`, compare exponents exactly
  (`RootReduce` and/or `FullSimplify`, never numerically), reject inexact
  input, return `Failure` objects, and supply an independent residual check
  by composing the forward model with the truncated inverse.

Other notable differences:

- **Normalization of the uniformizer.** Reports 01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 08, 09 use
  `z = (y/a)^{1/p}`; 03 works in `t = y/a` with `q = 1/p`; 07 has `t = y/a` and
  `p = 1`.  The formulas are equivalent after `D_t = D_z/p`.
- **Remainder object.** 01, 02 and 08 use an inert head
  (`PowerLogRemainder`, `PowerLogOrder`), 04 `PowerLogO`, 03/05/06/07/09 an
  association or a bare scale expression.  Inert heads prevent accidental
  arithmetic on the remainder.
- **Verification.** Python checks range from 18 (01) to 155 (09); MUnit suites
  from 33 (06) to 95 (02) tests.  No report ran its Wolfram code.

## 4. Errors and weak points found

- Report 07, §13.1, claims that a nonunit leading power `a x^p` forces "an
  infinite binomial expansion rather than a finite exact log-power sum".  This
  is false: the substitution `t = x^p`, `Φ(t) = t^{1/p}` in the Lagrange
  formula gives the finite exact coefficient formula with the factors
  `p^{−n}` and `p j` used by the other eight reports.
- Report 07's remainder degree `d ⌊s+/δ⌋` and report 08's `N d` with
  `N = ⌈B/δ*⌉` are valid but can be far from sharp (e.g. degree 6 reported
  where the true frontier degree is 3); reports 02–05 and 09 compute the exact
  degree of the complete first omitted block.
- Reports 01 and 04 (see the table above) let `Simplify` decide whether an
  algebraic residual coefficient vanishes; `RootReduce` and prime-factorised
  logarithms are required.
- Report 05's Theorem 5.1 (finite smoothness suffices for the marker
  formula) is correct but its inductive step is only sketched; the unified
  article gives a complete proof.
- Report 02's Theorem 4.1 asserts convergence of the `g^q` expansion while
  its convergence theorem is stated for `g` only; the extension is immediate
  but missing.
- Several reports (01, 04, 07, 09) require the package caller to keep the
  forward-remainder budget by hand; only 01, 05 and 06 expose it in the API.
- The `InverseResidual`-type helpers of 03 and 07 take an optional positional
  cutoff before `OptionsPattern[]`, so passing an option without the cutoff
  mis-binds the option as the cutoff.

## 5. What the unified work keeps

The [mathematical article](../../docs/article/asymptotic-inverse.tex) and the package originally
named AsymptoticInverse (now [AsymptoticAnalysis](../../src/README.md))
were written after this comparison and adopt:

- the marker (auxiliary-parameter) Lagrange–Bürmann theorem with a residue
  proof (01, 05, 06, 09), the Euler-operator coefficient formula with the
  observable power `r` built in (02, 09), and the composition recurrence
  `Q_{k+1} = ((a − k) Q_k + Q_k')/(k+1)` (06) as the single primitive of the
  jet algebra;
- the convergence proof by Rouché's theorem uniformly in the logarithm with
  Cauchy estimates (05, 06, 09), because it also yields the explicit
  conditional tail majorants of 02, 07 and 09;
- the first omitted block as an asymptotic *equality* with its exact
  logarithmic degree (02, 03, 04, 05), the boundary-set enumeration (01, 06,
  09), and the inert remainder head (01, 02, 08);
- the tame near-identity theorem of 05 (finite smoothness), with a complete
  proof, as the justification of `PerturbativeInverse` for perturbations
  outside the finite power–log class;
- the transport of forward remainders (01, 03, 05, 06) exposed as
  `"InputRemainder"`, and the residual-to-error bracket (01, 05, 06, 08);
- the Newton engine (06) as `Method -> "Newton"`, depth truncation for
  symbolic exponents (09), the Lambert-W core analysis (02, 04, 08) as the
  boundary of the theory, and the exclusive cutoff in the output variable
  (01–04, 06, 09).

The package additionally handles finite expansion points from either side and
both infinities (through signed leading powers `p ≠ 0`, which none of the nine
implement), expands arbitrary forward functions with a precision-tracking
power–log engine (which also answers the second question, on
`(1 + x + x^√2)^√2` at infinity), and converts rational-exponent results to
`SeriesData`.
