\begin{abstract}
This is an incremental source audit against a substantial existing review corpus,
not another enumeration of its backlog. The repository's review indexes and
consolidated registers cover 36 earlier reports and 231 attributed entries.
A broad inspection of core jets, observable operations, specialized asymptotic
adapters, interval arithmetic, and Mathics support leads to one independently
specified new high-priority soundness finding: the shared absolute-value
shortcut infers the reality of an entire finite approximation from the sign
of only its leading coefficient. Complex intermediate coefficients can then
cancel after the invalid nonlinear rewrite, leaving a real but incorrect
result labelled exact.

The report gives source-predicted public witnesses that respect the complex-literal
input restriction, exact mathematical countermodels, a fail-closed patch emitter,
eight pending Wolfram regression tests, and an independently implemented
complex-modulus jet model. The accompanying Python run passes 115 exact/model
and patch-fixture checks. These are not Wolfram package tests. No native package
execution or native performance improvement is claimed. A positive-real-coordinate
modulus extension and a Hermitian convolution prototype provide concrete
follow-on development work without recycling the earlier general roadmaps.
\end{abstract}
\vspace{8pt}
\noindent\textbf{Principal conclusion.}
Validate an operation's mathematical identity \emph{before} allowing later
coefficient cancellation to erase evidence that its preconditions failed.
For \code{Abs}, the minimal sound magnitude repair checks the complete
\emph{retained finite jet}; it need not require the unknown remainder itself
to be real.

\section{The relevant internal contract}
\subsection{Jets and uncertainty}
The ordinary precision algebra represents a local quantity by
\[
J=(T,P,D),\qquad
T=\{(\alpha_j,Q_j(L))\}_{j=1}^{m},\qquad L=\log u,\quad u\to0^+.
\]
Its finite approximation is
\[
 p(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u^{\alpha_j}Q_j(\log u),
\]
and finite \(P\) records a magnitude remainder
\[
 z(u)=p(u)+\OO\!\left(u^P(1+|\log u|)^D\right).
\]
An infinite precision marker represents an exact finite expression.
The core implements addition, multiplication, powers, logarithms, exponentials,
and composition in this representation.\cite{core}

The distinction between \emph{intermediate} and \emph{completed} coefficients
is important. The source explicitly permits complex intermediate summands
that cancel, and calls \code{realCoefficientRows} at representation boundaries.
The implementation of \code{seriesMake} performs that final validation before
building a public result. This is a reasonable policy for algebraic collection:
for example, \((1+az)+(1-az)=2\) is a valid identity regardless of whether \(a\)
is real.\cite{core,operations}

It is not, by itself, a policy for applying nonlinear functions to those
intermediates. A nonlinear rewrite needs its own precondition. One cannot
establish that a nonlinear step was valid merely by observing that the ultimate
coefficient vector is real.

\subsection{The literal-input screen is not a codomain proof}
The source's initial exact-input predicate is syntactic:
\begin{lstlisting}
exactQ[e_] := FreeQ[e, _Real | _Complex];
validateInput[f_, limit_] := (
  If[! exactQ[f], fail["InexactInput", ...]];
  ...);
\end{lstlisting}
It rejects literal complex-number atoms, including \code{I}, as well as
approximate real numbers. It does not prove that every symbolic intermediate
is real. A symbolic parameter \code{a} with the exact constraint
\code{a^2 == -1} contains neither a \code{Real} nor a \code{Complex} atom.
The input screen and the later coefficient-reality proof serve different
purposes.\cite{core}

Consequently, the most tempting illustrative call with literal \code{I} is
\emph{not} the public reproducer used in this report. That call is retained as
a negative control in the regression file. The actual proposed witness uses
a symbolic parameter, and its overall observable is real-valued on the
retained real input path.

\subsection{Where the precondition is lost}
The relevant observable flow is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\texttt{SeriesObservable}\longrightarrow \texttt{seriesJetApply}\longrightarrow \texttt{fwdAbs}\\
\longrightarrow \texttt{pAdd}/\texttt{pMul}\longrightarrow \texttt{seriesMake}.
\end{gathered}
\]
In \code{seriesJetApply}, \code{Abs} has a dedicated arm:
\begin{lstlisting}
h === Abs,
  fwdAbs[seriesJetApply[e[[1]], x, input, d, cut, limit],
    ell, ass]
\end{lstlisting}
It does not take the generic unary Taylor arm, where the current source checks
complete-argument reality, approach compatibility, and returned Taylor data.
The public wrapper validates the input expression, handles conditional
expressions, applies this dispatcher, and then builds the result. It does not
first replace the whole observable by a complex-modulus formula.\cite{operations}

That difference also explains the novelty relative to the preceding Taylor
reviews: no native Taylor oracle is needed to trigger the present mechanism.
An exact monomial input and finite algebraic intermediate jets suffice.

\section{ABS-01: the absolute-value shortcut is unsound on complex jets}
\subsection{The implementation}
The complete reviewed helper is small enough to show:
\begin{lstlisting}
fwdAbs[j : {T_, P_, D_}, ell_, ass_] := Module[{q, c, degree},
  If[T === {}, Return[j, Module]];
  q = T[[1, 2]]; degree = polyDegree[q, ell];
  c = (-1)^degree Coefficient[q, ell, degree];
  Which[provablyPositive[c, ass], j,
    provablyNegative[c, ass], pScale[j, -1, ell, ass],
    True, fail["UnprovedSign",
      "The eventual sign of the absolute-value argument could not be proved."]]];
\end{lstlisting}
This is \code{src/Kernel/AsymptoticAnalysis.wl}, lines 460--465 in the pinned
snapshot.\cite{core}

For a \emph{real} finite jet, the leading logarithmic coefficient determines
an eventual sign, since \(L\to-\infty\). Thus the factor \((-1)^d\) is sensible.
The missing hypothesis is that the complete finite approximation is real.
A real positive leading coefficient does not supply that hypothesis.
For instance, \(1+iu\) has leading coefficient 1 but
\[
 |1+iu|=\sqrt{1+u^2}\ne1+iu.
\]
The complex meaning of \code{Abs} is the ordinary modulus, not a formal
real-sign operator.\cite{absdoc}

\subsection{A public witness that respects the input screen}
Run the following in a fresh kernel with the pinned package loaded:
\begin{lstlisting}
Clear[x, z, a];
s = AsymptoticAnalysis`AsymptoticExpansion[
  x, {x, 0, 4},
  Assumptions -> a^2 == -1,
  "Backend" -> "Package"];

r = AsymptoticAnalysis`SeriesObservable[
  s, Abs[1 + a z] + Abs[1 - a z], z,
  "Cutoff" -> 4];

{Normal[r], r["Remainder"], r["Exact"], r["Assumptions"]}
\end{lstlisting}
\textbf{Source-predicted behavior, not a recorded native output:}
the finite expression becomes 2, the remainder becomes zero, and the exactness
flag becomes true. The parameter condition remains a condition on \(a\), not
a proof that \(a\) is real.

The symbolic condition is satisfiable over the complex numbers and says
\(a\in\{i,-i\}\). Both values give exactly the same real observable on real \(x\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
 F(x)=|1+ax|+|1-ax|=2\sqrt{1+x^2}.
\end{equation}
The source input is \(s(x)=x\), a real exact function; the observable \(F\) is
also real and nonnegative. This is not a request for a complex output series
or an expansion in a complex sector.

The constraint is useful because it removes any ambiguity about the intended
parameter domain. An unconstrained \(a\) would also fail to justify the shortcut,
but a user or reader might mistakenly infer an unstated real-parameter policy.
Here, the realness of the \emph{observable} is true while the realness of its
\emph{arguments to each modulus} is explicitly false away from zero.

\subsection{Source-level execution trace}
The ordinary exact input gives the internal jet
\[
 J_x=(\{(1,1)\},\infty,0).
\]
Before \code{Abs}, the two inner expressions give
\[
 J_+=(\{(0,1),(1,a)\},\infty,0),\qquad
 J_-=(\{(0,1),(1,-a)\},\infty,0).
\]
Their leading polynomial is 1. The reviewed helper therefore returns \(J_+\)
and \(J_-\) unchanged. Addition then cancels the \(a\)-dependent blocks:
\[
 J_++J_-=(\{(0,2)\},\infty,0).
\]
The final real-coefficient validator has nothing nonreal left to reject.
\code{seriesMake} associates an infinite remainder precision with zero remainder
and exactness. Every algebraic step \emph{after} the false \code{Abs} rewrite
is internally consistent; that is why a stronger final output-type check alone
would not repair the problem.\cite{core,operations}

This trace is also a useful debugging guide. Instrument the argument and return
value of \code{fwdAbs}, not just the final \code{GeneralizedSeries}. The loss of
information occurs before the evidence becomes invisible through cancellation.

\subsection{Exact contradiction and order of the error}
The correct local expansion of \eqref{eq:pair} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairseries}
 F(x)=2+x^2-\frac{x^4}{4}+\frac{x^6}{8}
       -\frac{5x^8}{64}+\OO(x^{10}).
\end{equation}
For the exclusive cutoff 4, an ordinary sound answer retains \(2+x^2\) and an
appropriate fourth-order remainder. A structured refusal is also sound if this
coefficient class is not implemented. The predicted exact constant 2 is not.

There is no numerical conditioning issue here. Rationalization gives the exact
identity
\[
 F(x)-2=\frac{2x^2}{\sqrt{1+x^2}+1},
\]
so
\[
 \lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{F(x)-2}{x^2}=1,
 \qquad
 \lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{F(x)-2}{x^4}=+\infty.
\]
Thus even attaching \(\OO(x^4)\) to the constant 2 would not repair the missing
coefficient. At the convenient exact point \(x=3/4\), \(F(x)=5/2\), whereas the
predicted constant is 2. The local limit, rather than this single point, is the
asymptotic proof; the rational point is an easily checked diagnostic.

\subsection{A polynomial-valued witness with a wrong coefficient sign}
A second observable eliminates even the square root from the exact answer:
\begin{lstlisting}
r2 = AsymptoticAnalysis`SeriesObservable[
  s, Abs[1 + a z]^2 + Abs[1 - a z]^2, z,
  "Cutoff" -> 4];
\end{lstlisting}
On the retained parameter condition,
\[
 |1+ax|^2+|1-ax|^2=2+2x^2.
\]
The same shortcut instead propagates
\[
 (1+ax)^2+(1-ax)^2=2+2a^2x^2=2-2x^2.
\]
The resulting wrong coefficients are real; the predicted error is exactly
\(4x^2\). This witness is valuable because neither the correct expression nor
the incorrectly rewritten one needs an infinite Taylor series.

Both witnesses should be kept: the first detects a lost term and false
exactness, while the second detects a real coefficient of the wrong sign.
They are two tests for ABS-01, not two separately counted findings.

\subsection{Additional consequences and controls}
Subtracting 2 from the first predicted result produces an exact zero in place
of a genuinely nonzero quadratic germ. Downstream arithmetic is then entitled
to eliminate this term or to report that it has no nonzero leading block.
Those consequences are caused by the original false exactness; they should
not be blamed on otherwise correct zero handling.

The difference
\[
 |1+ax|-|1-ax|=0
\]
provides a complementary symptom. The erroneous helper produces \(2ax\), which
can be rejected by the final real-coefficient check despite the correct
observable being identically zero. This shows why testing only isolated
\code{Abs} calls, where a nonreal output may be caught later, does not exercise
the most dangerous cancellation case.

The regression catalog also retains positive controls. Under
\code{Element[a, Reals]}, the first pair really is eventually equal to 2.
The functions \(|1+x|\) and \(|-1+x|\) have the expected real linear germs.
Ordinary algebraic cancellation without a nonlinear rewrite remains valid.
The literal-\code{I} spelling is expected to fail at input validation and is
explicitly not counted as a bug reproduction.

\section{The minimal repair and its proof}
\subsection{Guard the finite jet at the nonlinear boundary}
A conservative repair is to retain the fast sign implementation but establish
that every retained coefficient polynomial is real under the recorded
assumptions. The supplied emitter inserts the following check after the
empty-jet case and before the leading-sign computation:
\begin{lstlisting}
If[! AllTrue[T,
    TrueQ[realPolynomialCondition[#[[2]], ell, ass]] &],
  fail["UnprovedAbsJetRealness",
    "The absolute-value sign shortcut requires every retained coefficient to be provably real.",
    <|"FiniteJet" -> T, "Assumptions" -> ass|>]];
\end{lstlisting}
The existing coefficient predicate is already available in the core. The
proposal does not install a global restriction against all complex
intermediates, and it does not change \code{pConst}, \code{pAdd}, or legitimate
algebraic cancellation. It repairs the precondition at the operation that
requires it.\cite{core}

The new failure name is a proposed diagnostic, not an existing upstream API
promise. It identifies the exact reason the real-sign fast path was refused.
The full finite jet is included for diagnostic inspection; a production version
may prefer a compact offending-block payload, especially for large jets.

\subsection{Why a complete-source reality proof is unnecessary here}
A stronger-looking fix would require the full argument of \code{Abs}, including
its unknown error, to be real. That requirement is sufficient, but is not
necessary for a magnitude approximation.

\begin{theorem}[Sound real-finite-jet shortcut]\label{thm:realguard}
Let \(p(u)\) be a finite real power--log expression on a positive real local
coordinate. Suppose its first nonzero block establishes an eventual sign
\(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\). Let
\[
 z(u)=p(u)+R(u),\qquad
 |R(u)|\le C u^P(1+|\log u|)^D
\]
on a sufficiently small deleted neighborhood. The error \(R\) may be complex.
Then
\[
 |z(u)|=\sigma p(u)+\OO\!\left(u^P(1+|\log u|)^D\right).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The eventual sign gives \(|p(u)|=\sigma p(u)\). The reverse triangle inequality
in \(\mathbb C\) yields
\[
 \bigl||z(u)|-|p(u)|\bigr|\le|z(u)-p(u)|=|R(u)|.
\]
Combining these two statements proves the assertion with the same magnitude
majorant constant.
\end{proof}

The theorem explains the appropriate granularity of the guard. Reality must
hold for the complete \emph{retained approximation}, not just its leading
coefficient. But discarded complex information already covered by the
recorded error does not force a refusal. This matters when a later refinement
first reveals a previously omitted imaginary block: the weaker earlier
magnitude approximation can have been valid even though the refined sign-only
path must now refuse or use a true modulus expansion.

The empty-jet case is also sound for magnitudes: \(z=\OO(R)\) implies
\(|z|=\OO(R)\). The candidate preserves it. This is not a theorem about
arbitrary derivative contracts through a zero-crossing modulus. The supplied
reference model intentionally carries no derivative certificates, and the
candidate should not be presented as a general theorem about differentiating
nonsmooth observables.

\subsection{Behavior and compatibility of the candidate}
On ABS-01, the guard fails before \(a\) and \(-a\) can cancel. The proposed
minimal implementation therefore returns a structured refusal, not the
mathematically richer expansion \(2+x^2+\OO(x^4)\). That is an intentional
soundness-first repair. Restoring support for this valid real observable is
separate work, specified in Section~\ref{sec:modulus}.

For a real finite jet, the original sign logic remains unchanged. The cost is
one retained-coefficient reality pass at each \code{Abs} node. A later optimization
can reuse already established coefficient facts within the same assumptions
scope, but this audit does not claim a measured performance regression or
improvement from the guard.

The emitter requires one exact copy of the reviewed function and refuses an
absent, duplicated, modified, or already-patched anchor. It reads its input and
writes a unified diff to standard output. It does not mutate a checkout or
claim that matching one function establishes the revision identity of an
entire repository.

\section{A genuine modulus implementation on the real local coordinate}
\label{sec:modulus}
\subsection{The intended extension is smaller than complex asymptotics}
The useful next capability is not an unspecified complex-sector engine.
The coordinate remains \(u\to0^+\), all exponents remain real, and the completed
output remains real. Only intermediate finite coefficients are allowed to be
complex, followed by the well-defined map \(z\mapsto|z|\).

This directly supports real observables built from complex algebraic factors,
Euclidean norms, and conjugate pairs. It also avoids the unsatisfactory user
experience in which \(\sqrt{1+x^2}\) is supported while an equivalent modulus
expression is either refused or incorrectly treated as a real sign.
These are specific consequences of ABS-01 rather than a repetition of the
repository's existing general complex-extension roadmap.

\subsection{Normalize the finite approximation}
Assume the leading coefficient is a fixed, nonzero complex constant, independent
of \(L\). Write
\[
 p(u)=c u^\alpha(1+U(u)),\qquad c\ne0,
\]
where \(U\) is a finite complex power--log expression with positive valuation.
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H=U+\overline U+U\overline U.
\end{equation}
Then \(H\) is real, \(H\to0\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normunit}
 |p(u)|=|c|u^\alpha\sqrt{1+H(u)}.
\end{equation}
The eventual positive square-root branch is automatic from \(H\to0\); it is not
an arbitrary branch selection.

Conjugation here fixes the real coordinate and \(L=\log u\). It conjugates
coefficient scalars only. Substituting an unrelated complex conjugation rule
for \(u\), or treating \(L\) as a free complex variable, would solve a different
problem.

The simplest witness has \(c=1\), \(U=iu\), and \(H=u^2\). In particular, the
imaginary first-order perturbation disappears from \(U+\overline U\), but
reappears in the \emph{real quadratic} product \(U\overline U\). That is precisely
the contribution the reviewed sign shortcut loses.

\subsection{Finite truncation with an explicit logarithmic degree}
Let the requested absolute exponent cutoff be \(h\), and let the incoming
magnitude error be \(\OO(u^P\LL^D)\), where \(\LL=1+|\log u|\). Work only to
\[
 h_* = \min(h,P)
\]
when \(P\) is finite; otherwise take \(h_*=h\). Suppose \(h_*>\alpha\), and
suppose the finite expression \(H\) has positive valuation \(v\) and maximum
coefficient logarithmic degree \(d\). Define
\[
 K=\left\lceil\frac{h_*-\alpha}{v}\right\rceil.
\]
Compute
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitebinomial}
 |c|u^\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\binom{1/2}{k}H^k
\end{equation}
and retain only blocks of absolute exponent strictly below \(h_*\).

\begin{theorem}[Conservative modulus truncation]\label{thm:modulus}
Under these hypotheses, the retained expression \(M(u)\) satisfies
\[
 |z(u)|-M(u)
 =\OO(u^P\LL^D)+\OO(u^{h_*}\LL^{Kd}),
\]
with the first term omitted when the incoming finite approximation is exact.
The coefficients in \(M\) are real.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since \(H\to0\), the real function \(\sqrt{1+H}\) has a Taylor remainder
\(\OO(H^K)\) after degree \(K-1\). Each block of \(H\) has exponent at least
\(v\) and logarithmic degree at most \(d\), so
\[
 H=\OO(u^v\LL^d),\qquad
 u^\alpha H^K=\OO(u^{\alpha+Kv}\LL^{Kd})
              =\OO(u^{h_*}\LL^{Kd}).
\]
Every discarded finite product from \eqref{eq:finitebinomial} has exponent at
least \(h_*\) and logarithmic degree at most \((K-1)d\), so it obeys the same
conservative bound. The reverse triangle inequality transports the incoming
error from \(z\) to \(|p|\) without any division by \(p\). Finally, \(H\) is real
and its binomial coefficients are real, proving coefficient reality.
\end{proof}

The formula is intentionally conservative. It does not infer exactness because
the currently visible omitted coefficient vanishes. Sharper first-omitted-block
bounds can be added with an independent finite-support or tail argument, but
are not needed to repair ABS-01.

When \(h\le\alpha\), no leading block is requested; \(|p|=\OO(u^\alpha)\) supplies
a valid magnitude answer. When \(H=0\) exactly, \(|p|=|c|u^\alpha\) is an exact
finite modulus of the finite approximation, while any original error must
still be retained. The all-real finite-jet path can continue to use
Theorem~\ref{thm:realguard}, including real logarithmic leading polynomials.

\subsection{What the executable reference implements}
The delivered \code{code/modulus_jet.py} implements this magnitude construction
with sparse dictionaries, exact rational exponents, and SymPy polynomials in
a real symbol \(L\). Its coefficient scalars must be exact numeric expressions;
it deliberately refuses unproved symbolic-parameter cases rather than attempting
to reproduce Wolfram's assumption prover.

It supports real finite jets by eventual sign, complex log-independent leading
coefficients by \eqref{eq:normunit}, logarithmic perturbations, inherited magnitude
errors, exact zero, and cutoff clipping. It refuses unsupported complex
logarithmic leading amplitudes, inexact constants, inconsistent retained/error
orders, and exhausted operation budgets. It is neither a replacement package
nor a verified translation of all upstream jet operations.

A nonconstant complex leading logarithmic polynomial is a meaningful further
case, but its norm need not be polynomial in \(L\). For example,
\(|1+iL|=\sqrt{1+L^2}\). Supporting it requires another normalization or a richer
coefficient class. The prototype refuses this case explicitly; it does not
silently project its leading coefficient onto the real axis.

The reference initially forms the full finite product \(U\overline U\) subject
to its budget. That is an intentionally simple implementation choice, not a
claim that it has solved the repository's previously recorded general
truncation-performance issues.

\section{A specific performance opportunity: Hermitian pairing}
For a finite expansion
\[
 p(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u^{\alpha_j}c_j(L),
\]
forming \(p\overline p\) naively considers \(m^2\) ordered coefficient products.
The involution exchanging the two factors gives the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hermitian}
 p\overline p=
 \sum_i u^{2\alpha_i}|c_i|^2+
 2\sum_{i<j}u^{\alpha_i+\alpha_j}
       \operatorname{Re}(c_i\overline{c_j}).
\end{equation}
Thus the coefficient-product enumeration can use the upper triangle, with
\(m(m+1)/2\) pairs, while collecting equal summed weights normally.

The separate \code{hermitian_square} prototype implements this identity and
was compared with the ordinary ordered convolution. These are measured
\emph{operation counts inside the Python model}, not native timing results:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
Input support \(m\) & Ordered products & Paired products\\
\midrule
8 & 64 & 36\\
16 & 256 & 136\\
32 & 1024 & 528\\
64 & 4096 & 2080\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All four resulting exact polynomials matched. At support 64, the prototype
performs 2016 fewer candidate coefficient products. Both algorithms remain
quadratic, and real-part simplification has its own cost. These counts therefore
justify trying the specialization, not promising an approximately twofold
wall-clock speedup in Wolfram or Mathics.

An integrated implementation should pair before coefficient simplification,
use the same exact exponent-equality machinery as the ordinary jet algebra,
and only then collect equal resulting weights. One-sided complex coefficient
cancellation must not be confused with cancellation of independent unknown
errors: \eqref{eq:hermitian} concerns the \emph{finite approximation}, whose
algebraic correlations are known.

This optimization is particularly natural at the new modulus boundary. It is
not a request to rewrite all multiplication in the package. The delivered
prototype is separate from the main modulus routine so that its exact output
and product-count effect can be inspected without implying an already integrated
upstream optimization.

\section{Validation and reproducibility}
\subsection{What the 115 checks establish}
The executed checks include the two symbolic source-control-flow models and
their exact mathematical contradictions; a grid of imaginary perturbations
with rational exponents and several cutoffs; equality below the requested
cutoff against independent SymPy expansions; exact phase and conjugation
invariance; real, logarithmic, zero, and pure-error cases; input-precision clipping;
explicit unsupported-input and budget refusals; Hermitian convolution equality
and counts; and the patch emitter's fail-closed behavior.

The run is reproducible from the archive root:
\begin{lstlisting}
python code/independent_checks.py \
  --output evidence/independent_results.json
\end{lstlisting}
The JSON records the complete check list rather than only a success total.
\nolinkurl{evidence/independent_checks.txt} records the authoring run's summary.
The dependency is declared in \code{code/requirements.txt}; Python and SymPy
versions used for that run are stored in the JSON.

The case count is not a statistical claim, a formal proof of a general CAS,
or an upstream test count. Several checks deliberately share a mathematical
family. The general error statements are supplied by the proofs in this
article; the executable cases test their implementation and boundary behavior.

\subsection{Pending Wolfram tests}
The eight tests in \code{AbsGuardRegressions.wlt} specify the conservative
candidate's desired behavior, not an already accepted upstream contract.
The first two require the new structured refusal; the remaining six check
real-parameter, signed-real, cancellation, literal-input, and exact-zero controls.
A later full modulus implementation should replace the first two refusals with
correct coefficient and remainder assertions, not retain them as permanent
feature limitations.

To observe the baseline in a fresh kernel:
\begin{lstlisting}
wolframscript -file code/characterize.wl \
  /checkout/Asymptotic/AsymptoticAnalysis.wl
\end{lstlisting}
The script prints the kernel version, selected input entry, seed result,
observable results, and independent identities. A failed seed construction
terminates the script rather than pretending to have reached the target helper.
The exact message stream may differ from the source prediction; that is why the
observation script is separate from the desired-contract suite.

To emit the candidate diff:
\begin{lstlisting}
python code/abs_guard_patch.py \
  /checkout/Asymptotic/src/Kernel/AsymptoticAnalysis.wl \
  > abs-guard.patch
\end{lstlisting}
Review and apply that diff only to an appropriate scratch checkout. The upstream
source map states that the standalone must be regenerated from the modular
sources.\cite{sourcemap}
\begin{lstlisting}
python validation/build_standalone.py
python validation/build_standalone.py --check
wolframscript -file /path/to/review/code/run_focused.wl \
  /checkout/Asymptotic/AsymptoticAnalysis.wl
\end{lstlisting}
The driver requires all eight desired-contract tests to succeed for a zero exit
status. It has not been executed in a native kernel here. Nothing in this archive
is an instruction to run the upstream full suite contrary to the repository's
current development guidance.

\subsection{Integration sequence and acceptance conditions}
First characterize the pinned baseline and preserve its actual output. This
is especially useful for checking whether a surrounding evaluator normalization
changes the proposed public witness before it reaches the helper. If so, test
the helper invariant directly as well; do not replace a failed public
reproduction with an invented transcript.

Second, apply the finite-jet guard and establish that the two dangerous
cancellation cases either refuse at the intended boundary or return a
mathematically correct result through an independently justified path. Real
controls must remain valid. Test modular and regenerated standalone entries
in fresh processes so that earlier definitions do not mask the edited helper.

Third, implement the true modulus operation as a distinct semantic operation
and rerun the same witness family as positive functionality tests. The input
constraint \(a^2=-1\) is a useful parameter-proof case in Wolfram; the Python
prototype's \(a=i\) specialization is only its independent mathematical oracle.
A runtime that cannot prove the required coefficient facts should preserve a
clear refusal rather than accepting them numerically.

Finally, evaluate the paired convolution in the actual interpreter with
representative coefficient simplification costs. The exact pair-count reduction
is already established; the native cost model remains to be measured.
These are bounded acceptance steps tied to ABS-01, not a repeated general
project-wide validation roadmap.

\section{Relationship to earlier reviews and limits of the conclusions}
\subsection{Why this is not a restatement of the preceding findings}
The nearest earlier entries concern final coefficient reality, the real-domain
premises of some operations, and generic native observable Taylor admission.
The present source already contains the final real-coefficient guard and the
strengthened generic Taylor arm.\cite{status,wave3,operations}

ABS-01 survives those protections for two specific reasons: \code{Abs} does not
use the generic unary arm, and the invalid intermediate terms cancel to a
\emph{real} final coefficient vector. No inaccurate native order, missing
Taylor coefficient, opaque custom function, incomplete native result, or
rounded numerical test is involved. The needed repair is the precondition of
this shared modulus shortcut, not another final-output realness check.

The selected original ledgers identify their related findings as constant
coefficient admission, discarded complex information affecting a trigonometric
bound, and generic Taylor order/approach behavior. They were used to delimit the
new mechanism, not copied into the new finding list.\cite{review7,review12,review22,review25}
The novelty ledger in the archive records these relationships. The review does
not claim to have reread every paragraph of all 36 archived articles; it uses
their consolidated inventories and a targeted original-source cross-check.

\subsection{Other inspected areas}
The surrounding inspection was useful even where it did not yield another new
finding. The Barnes logarithm module explicitly attaches a finite Bernoulli
tail and uses positive-argument recurrence reductions. The Dirichlet adapter
contains an integral-comparison Zeta bound and a summed Taylor-moment Lerch
bound. The interval evaluator uses exact rational directed rounding in its
arithmetic primitives. These implementation facts informed the review scope;
they are not presented as new certifications of those subsystems.\cite{barnes,dirichlet,certificates}

Likewise, reading Mathics-specific assumption, algebra, Taylor, and call adapters
does not establish parity with Wolfram. The service failures prevented a fresh
runtime comparison. Existing compatibility issues recorded in the intake
register were not counted again merely because their code appeared in the
inspection.\cite{wave4,sourcemap}

Some private-helper concerns would require an additional violated call-site
precondition to become a demonstrated public defect. They were not promoted
to findings just to lengthen the report. The resulting small finding count
reflects the explicit nonduplication requirement and the evidence threshold,
not a claim that the rest of the repository is bug-free.

\subsection{Conclusions}
The immediate change is small: reject a nonreal retained finite jet before
using the real-sign implementation of \code{Abs}. The mathematical reason is
also small, but important: the sign of a leading coefficient is not a codomain
proof for all retained terms, and final cancellation cannot repair an invalid
nonlinear identity.

The constructive continuation is well specified. Compute the modulus of the
finite approximation through its Hermitian norm, carry the original error by
the reverse triangle inequality, and bound the additional finite truncation
with an explicit logarithmic degree. This produces real coefficients on the
same positive real coordinate, without promising a general complex asymptotic
engine. The paired convolution offers a concrete, exactly checked implementation
optimization for that operation.

The delivered patch is a candidate; the independent model is executed; the
Wolfram regressions remain pending. Keeping those three statements separate is
part of making the result actionable rather than overstated.
