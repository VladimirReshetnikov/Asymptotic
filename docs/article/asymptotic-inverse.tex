\title{Real asymptotic expansions and inverse functions\\[0.4em]
\large Power--logarithmic series, real branches, and error transport}
\author{Vladimir Reshetnikov\thanks{The initial manuscript was prepared with Claude (Anthropic).}}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Real powers and logarithms lead naturally beyond Taylor and Puiseux series.
For example, the positive inverse of $x+x^2(1+\log x)$ begins
$y-y^2(1+\log y)+y^3(2\log^2y+5\log y+3)$, whereas the inverse of
$x+x^{\sqrt2}$ begins $y-y^{\sqrt2}+\sqrt2\,y^{2\sqrt2-1}$.
We develop a calculus of complete power--logarithmic blocks with real
exponents at finite endpoints and at infinity.

Marker Lagrange--B\"urmann inversion and an Euler-operator formula give the
coefficients of arbitrary real powers of an inverse. Real branch selection,
weighted truncation, and error transport are treated explicitly. The inverse
series converges for the analytic finite-parameter class; separate theorems
justify finite-order expansions under derivative bounds. Exact changes of
coordinate extend the calculus to logarithmic hierarchies, retained inverse
cores, finite flat sectors, and Fourier-valued coefficients.

Special-function examples distinguish convergent defining expansions from
Poincar\'e tails and exact identities. Absolute error envelopes support finite
composition in the presence of exponential factors and oscillation. Gamma
and Barnes functions admit logarithmic normalization and increasing inverse
expansions around exact Lambert balances, with complete polynomial
reciprocal-logarithmic coefficients and finite-order error bounds.
Rational interval estimates turn residual bounds into rigorous real root
enclosures. Throughout, formal identities, analytic remainders, convergence,
and numerical certificates retain their separate hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
